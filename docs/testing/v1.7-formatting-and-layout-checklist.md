# v1.7 — formatting in the box, and the window in three columns

Twenty minutes, and the last row is the only one that needs a second machine. Nothing about rooms, sharing or files
changed; the input area, the tools on it, the faces in it and the shape of the window did.

| # | step | expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Open a room and look at the window. | **Three columns**: Rooms on the left, Members on the right, the conversation down the middle. The tool strip is above the message box and **inside** the middle column - it does not run over the rooms list or the members list. |
| 2 | Type `ordinary`, highlight `ordinary`, click **B**, send. | The word arrives **bold**, in your transcript and in the other machine's. The words round it are ordinary, and so is the `[time] name` headline over the message. |
| 3 | Click into your bold word with the caret, then out of it again. | The **B** button lights when the caret is in bold and goes flat when it leaves. The buttons tell you where you are, not where you last clicked. |
| 4 | Highlight a word, click **B**, then click **I** (same selection). | It comes out **bold italic**. Losing the bold here is the old RichTextBox trap and would be a regression. |
| 5 | Click **Aa ▼**. | A menu drops down with **Small / Normal / Large**, and each of the three words is written at its own size - the small one really is smaller. The one the caret is sitting in has the tick. |
| 6 | Pick **Large**, write a word, send. Then put the caret in it and open the menu again. | The tool is now **drawn large**, the message arrives large, and the menu says **Large** with the tick. Small is a footnote and large is emphasis; both readable. |
| 7 | Click **A ▼**. | The **whole palette** is there - red, orange, green, blue, purple, pink, automatic - and each colour is written in itself, so you are not reading a name and hoping. |
| 8 | Pick a colour, send; then pick **Automatic**, send. | The first arrives in that colour. The second carries **no colour at all**: it looks exactly like a message would have before v1.7 existed, in the reader's own colours. |
| 9 | Bold a word, send it, then type a plain sentence. | The plain sentence is **plain**. Emphasis that survives the send it was typed for is how a chat turns into a ransom note. |
| 10 | Look at the two buttons at the right end of the strip. | One is a **face** and one is a **sheet of paper**, and both are whole: the face is not cut across the top or the chin, and the page has its fold. No words on either - hover, and the tooltip says what each one is. |
| 11 | Open the emoji table and compare a face in it with the same face in a message you send. | Same face, **complete in both** - nothing missing at the edge of the picture, in the toolbar, the table or the transcript. Faces are slightly smaller than they were, and all of each one is there. |
| 12 | Press the face button, pick a face. | The table opens over the input area, the face lands **at the caret** in the box, and the table closes. Pressing Escape leaves the box exactly as it was. |
| 13 | Copy a sentence out of Word or a web page - one with a colour and a background - and press **Ctrl+V** in the box. | **Only the words arrive.** No fonts, no colours, no grey background, no size change. The box is for a chat message. |
| 14 | Press Print Screen (so the clipboard holds a picture, not words), then **Ctrl+V**. | Nothing is pasted and the status bar says so, naming the **page** button as the way to send a picture. |
| 15 | Open the window on a machine that already has something on the clipboard, then enter a room. | The message box is **empty**. Entering a room must never paste what was left on the clipboard. |
| 16 | Type or paste well over 500 characters, including a few faces like 🙂. | The box keeps **500 characters** (a face is one, not two), the cut lands on a whole character, and the status bar says the rest did not fit. |
| 17 | Drag the window down to its smallest size. | The tools stay on **one row** and none of them sits on top of another. Click **Underline**: it underlines - it does not open the emoji table. |
| 18 | Drag the window taller. | The **transcript** grows. The tool strip and the message box stay the same height, and Send stays beside the box, on the box's row. |
| 19 | As host: resize the window narrow, then look at the rooms header. | The **New** / **Delete** buttons stay inside the Rooms column, over the list - never over the transcript. |
| 20 | A screen reader, or just Tab round the strip. | Every tool says what it is: the toggles by their letters, the two menus by **font size** / **font colour**, the face as **Emoji** and the page as **Share a picture or a file**. |
| 21 | Enter a room and look at the empty message box. | The grey word **Message** is written in it — and goes the moment you type the first character, without the sentence jumping. (This row is new because the word had been in the code since the input area was, and had never once reached the screen.) |
| 22 | Type 🙂 😀 👍 👨‍👩‍👧 🇻🇳 into the box. | Every one is the **coloured** face — the same artwork as the emoji table and the transcript — with **no black outline** anywhere round it and no dark shoulder sticking out to its right. One press of Backspace removes one face, family and flag included: they are one character each. |
| 23 | Write `a🙂b`, look closely at it, then select the face. | The letters either side are **whole** — `b` has its first strokes, nothing is rubbed out beside a face — and the face sits **on the line** where the character is — level with the letters either side, neither riding above them nor sunk below, and not a few pixels to their left. Selecting it puts it on the **same blue** as any other selected text; click out of the box and the selection and the blue both go, leaving the face clean. |
| 24 | On **one line**, write a face; with the face still on that line, pick **Large** and write words, then **Small** and write another face. | They all stand on the **same floor**: the ordinary face is not riding near the top of the line with its outline showing under it, and the small one is not sunk below it. Send it: the transcript draws each face at the size it was written at - the large one bigger than the sentence, the small one smaller - not all three alike. |
| 25 | Pick **Large**, type a face, look at it, send it. | The large face is large, coloured, and outline-free in the box; the message arrives in the transcript with the same face at the same size. Paste a face in from somewhere else and it comes out coloured too — it is the same character either way. |
| 26 | Second machine: send formatted text both ways, and switch rooms so the transcript is rebuilt from history. | Both windows put the **same ink** on the screen - same bold, same size, same colour - and the formatting survives the rebuild. An ordinary message from the old version still arrives exactly as it always did. |
| 27 | On **one line**, type a face, pick **Large**, type a few words, go back to ordinary size and type three or four more faces one after another. | The box **keeps still**. Nothing flashes, strobes or blinks beside the caret while you type - what you have already written stays put. (New because the box used to measure the line from inside its own painting of it, and every measurement asked for another painting.)
| 28 | Send **🙂😀👍** as a single message with nothing between the faces, then send the same three with words between them. | The transcript shows **three** faces both times, and they are the three you sent - not one face drawn three times, and not one face standing in for the other two. |
| 29 | On **one line**, write words, pick **Large** and write more words, then put a face at the ordinary size and another at **Small** on the same line. Send it. | In the transcript every face **stands on the same line as the words beside it** - no face floats above the big words, and the small one does not ride high above the ordinary ones. Look at the bottoms: faces and letters stop together. |
| 30 | Open the **Emoji** table and look along its **last row**, and at the row ends of every row. | It ends where it ends now - no joined family and no flag at the end of the table - and **every tile shows one face**, not several strangers in a row and not two letters. |
| 31 | Share a photo, then double-click it in the transcript - **once near its left edge, once in the middle, once near its right edge**. Then **right-click** it near the right edge. | The viewer window opens from **all three** places on the photo, and the menu with **Save picture to…** appears from the right-hand side too. Double-clicking the empty strip **under** a tall photo still opens nothing. |
| 32 | A face is a blank with a face on it, not an outline with a face on it | Type `hello 🙂 world` in the box. Zoom in: there is no black-and-white face anywhere under the colour one, and no ring of outline left around its edge. Type a letter straight after the face - it is visible, in the colour the caret is sitting in. Choose red from the colour tool, click a face in the picker, keep typing: the letters are red. Paste a face in (Ctrl+V) and undo (Ctrl+Z) - no outline shows in either state | [ ] |
