# v1.7 — formatting in the box, and the window in three columns

Ten minutes, and the last row is the only one that needs a second machine. Nothing about rooms, sharing or files
changed; the input area and the shape of the window did.

| # | step | expected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Open a room and look at the window. | **Three columns**: Rooms on the left, Members on the right, the conversation down the middle. The tool strip is above the message box and **inside** the middle column - it does not run over the rooms list or the members list. |
| 2 | Type `ordinary`, highlight `ordinary`, click **B**, send. | The word arrives **bold**, in your transcript and in the other machine's. The words round it are ordinary, and so is the `[time] name` headline over the message. |
| 3 | Click into your bold word with the caret, then out of it again. | The **B** button lights when the caret is in bold and goes flat when it leaves. The buttons tell you where you are, not where you last clicked. |
| 4 | Highlight a word, click **B**, then click **I** (same selection). | It comes out **bold italic**. Losing the bold here is the old RichTextBox trap and would be a regression. |
| 5 | Click **Aa** and send; click it again and send; again. | The tool cycles normal → large → small, is **drawn at the size it is about to give**, and the message arrives that size. Small is a footnote, large is emphasis - both readable. |
| 6 | Click **A** until the button goes red, type, send. Then click **A** until it is plain again, type, send. | The first arrives red. Cycling back to the box's own colour sends **no colour at all**: the second message looks exactly like a message would have before v1.7 existed. |
| 7 | Bold a word, send it, then type a plain sentence. | The plain sentence is **plain**. Emphasis that survives the send it was typed for is how a chat turns into a ransom note. |
| 8 | Press **Emoji** on the strip, pick a face. | The table opens over the input area, the face lands **at the caret** in the box, and the table closes. Pressing Escape leaves the box exactly as it was. |
| 9 | Copy a sentence out of Word or a web page - one with a colour and a background - and press **Ctrl+V** in the box. | **Only the words arrive.** No fonts, no colours, no grey background, no size change. The box is for a chat message. |
| 10 | Press Print Screen (so the clipboard holds a picture, not words), then **Ctrl+V**. | Nothing is pasted and the status bar says so, naming **Share** as the way to send a picture. |
| 11 | Open the window on a machine that already has something on the clipboard, then enter a room. | The message box is **empty**. Entering a room must never paste what was left on the clipboard. |
| 12 | Type or paste well over 500 characters, including a few faces like 🙂. | The box keeps **500 characters** (a face is one, not two), the cut lands on a whole character, and the status bar says the rest did not fit. |
| 13 | Drag the window down to its smallest size. | The tools stay on **one row** and none of them sits on top of another. Click **Underline**: it underlines - it does not open the emoji table. |
| 14 | Drag the window taller. | The **transcript** grows. The tool strip and the message box stay the same height, and Send stays beside the box, on the box's row. |
| 15 | As host: resize the window narrow, then look at the rooms header. | The **New** / **Delete** buttons stay inside the Rooms column, over the list - never over the transcript. |
| 16 | Second machine: send formatted text both ways, and switch rooms so the transcript is rebuilt from history. | Both windows put the **same ink** on the screen - same bold, same size, same colour - and the formatting survives the rebuild. An ordinary message from the old version still arrives exactly as it always did. |
